# Pivot: The Online Y Combinator

*2026-07-05 — direction update on top of [VISION.md](VISION.md).*

## The new thesis

A place where aspiring developers, creators and founders from around the world — of different ages — meet, form teams, and build businesses in the cloud together, with AI as a co-builder. Community + collaborative build environment + a leaderboard of the most popular projects + support for building and scaling a business. "The online Y Combinator."

## What carries over from the current platform (a lot)

- **Browser-based collaborative build environment** — microStudio already has real-time multi-user editing, project invites, and cloud projects. This is the core of "build together in the cloud" and is already running.
- **AI co-builder** — the AI panel (chat → working code, one-click apply) ships today, with free-tier providers (Gemini/Groq/OpenRouter) configured.
- **Community + rankings** — public projects, likes, comments, and hot/top/new ranking already exist server-side; the leaderboard surfaces them competitively.
- **Verified tier** — repurposes cleanly from "understands their code" to founder credibility: a Verified badge signals a real, working, understood product — the platform's version of "YC-vetted."

## What genuinely changes

- **Audience**: students → builders/founders of all ages. Tone shifts from classroom to startup community.
- **Scope of what gets built**: games → products/businesses. Near-term the engine still builds games and interactive apps; the roadmap needs general web-app capability (or positioning: "start with games and interactive products — the fastest thing to ship and rank").
- **Support layer** (the "YC" part): none of this exists yet — team formation, mentorship, startup curriculum ("how to talk to users," "how to price"), demo days (game jams generalize well here), and eventually funding paths.

## The one landmine to decide early

The 400-voice panel's sharpest finding still applies, amplified: **mixing minors and adults in one community with business/money incentives is the classic trust-and-safety failure** (payment + private contact with minors is a grooming vector; child-labor law applies to minor founders). Options:
1. **18+ platform** — simplest, kills the edtech wedge entirely.
2. **Age-gated tracks** — one platform, separate spaces: under-18 build in moderated team spaces with no DMs and non-cash recognition; 18+ get the full founder experience including monetization. Leaderboard can be shared (it's public content).
3. Two brands on one infrastructure.

Recommendation: **option 2** — it keeps the learning-hub work as the under-18 track ("junior founders"), keeps schools as a distribution channel, and gives graduates somewhere to go at 18. This is also a story no competitor has: the platform that grows builders from first game to first company.

## Build order

1. **Leaderboard** (now) — public ranked page of top projects: competition + discovery + shareability.
2. Team/founder profiles — who's building what, looking-for-cofounder flags.
3. Startup-mode projects — pitch section (problem, users, plan) on the project page next to the game/app itself.
4. Demo days — scheduled jams with themes, panel-judged + leaderboard-judged.
5. Builder curriculum — the AI assistant grows "business mentor" prompts alongside code help.
6. Monetization rails (18+ only, per the panel's guardrails).
