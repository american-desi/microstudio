# Panel Review — 400 Simulated Voices on the Learning-Hub Plan

*A simulated global focus panel: 100 experts (learning science, child safety/policy, game industry, edtech/procurement), 100 teachers, 100 parents, and 100 students across North America, Europe, Asia, Africa, the Middle East, and Latin America — run as 16 cohorts of 25 voices each, July 2026. Simulated by AI; treat as structured hypothesis, validate with real users.*

## Overall verdict

The panel believes a narrowed version of this plan can work — and many across all four groups actively want it to exist — but not the plan as written. The consensus condition is threefold. First, the comprehension gate must move: it is the plan's most original asset as a credential and its most lethal liability as a publish blocker (it kills the core dopamine loop for students, false-negatives ELL/IEP/shy kids, is legally high-risk automated decision-making about children in the EU, and will be jailbroken by the most test-hardened adversaries on earth). Second, Phase 1 must be rebuilt around the schools and households that actually exist — one launch domain instead of 'all subjects,' honest unit economics that price human moderation, school plumbing (rostering, DPAs, GDPR), offline/mobile/low-bandwidth capability, and national-exam alignment — because parents, district CTOs, and ministries hold vetoes the current plan never addresses. Third, minor monetization and paid peer tutoring must come off the roadmap, and Phases 3-4 must come out of the operating plan, because their mere presence poisons the trust Phase 1 depends on. Do those three things and roughly two-thirds of a hostile-leaning 400-person panel converts to conditional supporters; the pedagogy itself was almost never the objection.

## What the panel validated (keep these)

- The anti-chocolate-covered-broccoli stance and per-subject mechanic matching — the single most universally praised element across all 16 cohorts; multiple skeptics said it alone was why they kept listening. Do not soften it.
- Learn-by-making as the core loop ('publishing is the new book report') — learning scientists confirmed the protégé effect has among the strongest effect sizes in the literature; teachers, parents, and younger students all found the making loop credible even when they doubted the playing loop.
- The 70%+ creator revenue share vs Roblox's ~25-30% — cited unprompted by teen creators, creator-economy operators, and Global South parents as the strongest hook in the plan; even opponents of minor monetization credited the ethics of the number. Keep the number; fix the rails.
- Free for individual kids with schools paying — equity-minded teachers, low-income parents, and Global South cohorts said this is the only pricing structure under which their children participate at all.
- Browser-based open-source engine on low-spec hardware — runs on school Chromebooks where Roblox is firewalled; the forkable/self-hostable base is a genuine procurement advantage in Germany, France, Nordics, and ministry contexts.
- Classroom-wedge-before-consumer sequencing — platform-mortality veterans confirmed consumer-first against Roblox with zero content dies in months.
- Naming the kill risks (child safety, wrong-physics UGC, inference cost, Roblox Education) in the founder's own deck — procurement veterans and safety skeptics said this self-awareness is why they engaged seriously at all.
- The comprehension check AS AN IDEA — Asian parents called it the first mechanic that fights 'AI did my homework' instead of enabling it; T&S veterans called it 'a rate limiter and provenance system disguised as pedagogy.' Its placement, not its existence, is the problem.

## Critical risks

### 1. The comprehension gate as a hard publish/no-publish blocker

**Risk:** The comprehension gate as a hard publish/no-publish blocker — psychometrically unvalidated, adversarially gameable (jailbreak scripts predicted within weeks by students themselves), demotivating at the exact dopamine moment, systematically false-negative for ELL/dyslexic/IEP/shy students, and plausibly unlawful in the EU as Annex III high-risk automated educational assessment of children with no appeal path

**Raised by:** All 16 cohorts — learning scientists, safety/policy specialists, game-industry veterans, district CTOs, every teacher cohort, every student cohort, and multiple parent cohorts

**Consequence:** Students churn to Scratch/Roblox where publish is one click; districts face IDEA/504 complaints on the first wrongly-failed accommodated student; EU market is closed on day one; once the gate is publicly fooled, the 'verified' brand — the platform's entire trust asset — collapses

### 2. Minor monetization and paid peer tutoring (Phase 2 as designed)

**Risk:** Minor monetization and paid peer tutoring (Phase 2 as designed) — a grooming vector (payment + private contact channel is the exact pattern in child-exploitation case files), child-labor/Coogan/Werbeverbot legal exposure varying by jurisdiction, and no payout rails minors can actually use

**Raised by:** Child-safety specialists (unanimous), parents in all four regions (majority dealbreaker in NA and EU), teachers in all regions, district CTOs, game-industry T&S veterans

**Consequence:** Parents and school boards veto the ENTIRE platform because the roadmap exists on the website; one money-plus-minor incident ends the schools business per the plan's own risk register

### 3. Inverted unit economics

**Risk:** Inverted unit economics: $1-3k/school (a $200-600k Year-1 ARR ceiling) against per-student AI inference, multi-turn comprehension examination, and the human moderation payroll a child-UGC platform legally requires — 'content scales with users, not payroll' is false because the moderation queue scales with users too, and the comprehension gate is a pedagogy gate, not a safety gate (it catches the kid who doesn't understand fractions, not the adult who understands children)

**Raised by:** Game-industry CFOs/T&S, edtech founders and procurement veterans, safety specialists, learning scientists

**Consequence:** Negative gross margin on every customer; safety pipeline gets silently underfunded, producing exactly the incident that kills the schools business — this is where the company dies first

### 4. The plan's school is imaginary outside well-connected US classrooms

**Risk:** The plan's school is imaginary outside well-connected US classrooms: no offline/low-bandwidth mode, no mobile creation, English-only AI, US-standards-only knowledge graph, dollar pricing public schools legally cannot buy, no GDPR (COPPA named, GDPR never mentioned — caught within minutes by three separate European parents), no exam alignment to WAEC/JEE/gaokao/suneung/ENEM/GCSE, and China silently absent from Asia-scale DAU math

**Raised by:** All Global South cohorts (teachers, parents, students Africa/MEA/LatAm/Asia), European teachers and parents, learning scientists, safety regulators

**Consequence:** The 'hub for everyone' structurally serves elite schools only; ministries, Datenschutzbeauftragte, and exam-anxious parents veto; the markets where 20M DAU actually lives are unreachable by the product as architected

### 5. The knowledge-graph profile is a longitudinal cognitive dossier of a minor

**Risk:** The knowledge-graph profile is a longitudinal cognitive dossier of a minor — every misconception, pace, and gap, built partly from AI chat logs, persisting from age 8 onward, with no stated retention, deletion, portability, ownership, or no-model-training guarantee

**Raised by:** Safety/privacy specialists, EU and NA parents, district compliance officers; students added it is 'a report card with an avatar' and a parent-nagging vector

**Consequence:** GDPR/COPPA enforcement exposure, school DPO vetoes across half of Europe, and the flagship product feature becomes the flagship compliance liability

### 6. The content flywheel conflates what the maker learned with what the player learns

**Risk:** The content flywheel conflates what the maker learned with what the player learns: median student games are quiz-reskins nobody voluntarily plays, there is no seeded quality floor or curation layer, and Phases 3-4 ('compete for hours' with TV apps and a handheld) contaminate Phase 1 trust — engagement-maximization aimed at children is the exact pattern parents, regulators, and phone-ban politics are converging against

**Raised by:** Learning scientists, game-industry veterans, students in all regions ('one boring session and I never return'), parents in all regions, safety specialists

**Consequence:** First-session boredom brands the platform 'school software' forever; districts read Phase 3 and see an attention-economy company in an education costume; the valuation story rests on the weaker loop

### 7. Missing school adoption plumbing and unpriced teacher labor

**Risk:** Missing school adoption plumbing and unpriced teacher labor: no Clever/ClassLink/LTI rostering, no DPA/SDPC templates, content filters that block UGC-plus-chat platforms by default, no time-boxed curriculum units for 45-minute periods, and 'educator review tiers' that quietly mean unpaid teacher weekends

**Raised by:** Teachers in all four regions (near-unanimous), district CTOs, procurement veterans

**Consequence:** The 200-schools Year-1 target is fiction — the product is legally and logistically un-adoptable before pedagogy is ever discussed, and the teachers who must champion it become the ones it exploits

## Tensions between groups (tradeoffs, not averages)

- Instant publish vs. gated publish: students (all regions) say publishing IS the reward and will churn at any gate; learning scientists say the low sharing floor is the constructionist engine; but Asian parents and safety experts LIKE the gate as proof and rate-limiter. Resolution is placement, not compromise: ungated publish to class/friends, gated 'Verified' tier for marketplace/credit/money.
- Two faces of one brand: students need the product to never say 'learning' out loud (social cost at ages 11-16 is fatal to voluntary use), while parents and teachers need it to visibly BE learning — students themselves called the school license 'a permission-laundering machine,' as a compliment. The product must show curriculum to adults and creator status to kids from the same data; averaging produces a product neither group accepts.
- 70% creator share vs. money-near-minors: the revenue share is the single strongest hook for teen creators and aspirational Global South families, and simultaneously a consent-killing red line for NA/EU parents, safety NGOs, and every school system. You cannot have both on one surface; the panel's resolution is non-cash credential ladders for minors and a firewalled 18+ economy.
- More hours vs. fewer hours: the north star (verified learning HOURS) and Phase 3 (TV apps, 20M DAU) optimize for exactly what screen-time-anxious parents, Brazil's phone ban, China's playtime caps, and the EU's addictive-design scrutiny are fighting. Teachers can be sold a mastery tool or parents a Roblox competitor — 'one of those stories is a lie.'
- Constructionist making vs. exam-calendar time economics: the making loop needs 10-40 discretionary hours; students in Seoul, Kota, Cairo, and exam-year Europe own 2-5 per week. The flywheel's content supply will come disproportionately from low-pressure systems, producing games misaligned with high-pressure curricula — breaking the playing loop for the largest student populations.
- Safety vs. sociality: kids only play where friends are (co-presence, multiplayer, chat), which is precisely the surface parents and COPPA/DSA compliance want closed. A platform with no together-mode loses the bedroom; one with open chat loses the school. The narrow path is COPPA-safe structured co-play (class multiplayer, squad creation, no open DMs).
- Teachers as free labor vs. the no-payroll thesis: content accuracy at UGC scale requires educator review, but unpaid review breaks teachers and paid review breaks 'content scales with users, not payroll.' Multiple cohorts independently resolved this the same way: make teachers PAID first-class creators and reviewers — which converts the thesis's biggest lie into its distribution strategy.
- COPPA-as-moat: praised by two US pragmatists as honest strategy, disqualifying in tone for every regulator in the room — compliance framed as a weapon rather than a duty of care, and factually backwards since the burden crushes the underfunded newcomer, not Roblox.

## Amendments

### A1 · phase first-build · do-before-first-build

Unbundle the gate from publishing: anyone publishes instantly to a class/friends/unlisted tier; the AI comprehension dialogue gates only the 'Verified Creator' badge, marketplace featuring, knowledge-graph credit, cross-school distribution, and (later, 18+) monetization. Reframe passing as leveling up a creator rank, never as a test that can fail publicly.

*Why:* The single most convergent finding across all 16 cohorts: students churn at a publish gate, constructionists call it the anti-Papert move, and safety/procurement experts only need the gate on the trust tier. Gating the credential preserves everything the gate is for while removing everything it breaks.

### A2 · phase first-build · do-before-first-build

Redesign the comprehension check itself: debugging-based assessment (hand the student a deliberately broken variant of their own game and see if they diagnose the concept-linked bug) instead of an oral exam; private, retryable failure with no public fail state; mandatory teacher override and plain-language rationale for every decision; accommodation modes (ELL, IEP/504, native-language assessment); audit logging and a human appeal path; published error rates by language background and age; and a paid youth red team (13-17) whose job is to jailbreak it before launch.

*Why:* Learning scientists say debugging is harder to game and better evidence; teachers refuse accountability for an opaque AI verdict; EU counsel says contestability and human oversight are legally required (AI Act Annex III); students in every region cheerfully described exactly how they will jailbreak a Q&A gate.

### A3 · phase 1 · do-before-first-build

Publish the honest unit-economics model before building: cost per student per year for inference (tutoring + examination + verification), human moderation cost per 1,000 published games, and safety headcount — against realistic pricing. Then reprice: district-level licenses ($15-50k with rostering, support, and compliance baked in) in the US, and purchasing-power-adjusted per-student pricing with ministry/NGO channels elsewhere. Drop the $1-3k dead-zone price and the 'COPPA burden = moat' language entirely.

*Why:* Game-industry CFOs, district procurement veterans, and safety leads independently identified the same fatal math: $4-12/student/year revenue against inference plus moderation payroll is negative margin per customer, and the moat framing both misreads the cost structure and offends every regulator who read it.

### A4 · phase 2 · do-before-first-build

Remove paid peer tutoring between minors and minor revenue splits from the roadmap (including the public website) — replace with a non-cash ladder: verified-creator certificates, portable portfolio credentials formatted for university/DSA/CTE applications, cosmetic status earned from knowledge nodes, and prize-pool game jams. Firewall the 18+ creator economy into a separate consumer surface with its own brand. Revisit minor earnings only with guardian-controlled wallets, Coogan-style accounts, and per-jurisdiction legal review.

*Why:* The sharpest veto in the panel: safety NGOs call the tutoring-plus-payment channel 'building the introduction service,' EU/NA parents withhold consent because the roadmap exists, and every teacher cohort said money features retroactively poison the school product. The credential ladder captures most of the motivational value at none of the legal risk.

### A5 · phase 1 · phase-1

Build the safety pipeline as a first-class, separately budgeted system distinct from the comprehension gate: human pre-publication review for all content reachable by under-13s, no open chat/DMs for minors at launch (structured, moderated class-multiplayer instead), remix-chain provenance scanning, CSAE detection and mandatory-reporting workflows, and a parent/teacher-visible review log per game ('moderation as transparency'). Publish moderation staffing ratios and SLAs as sales collateral.

*Why:* T&S veterans, parents, and district officers were unanimous: the comprehension gate checks pedagogy, not predation, and no district pilot happens without a legible safety architecture. The panel's safety hawks noted the first kids' UGC platform to make moderation visible wins district trust — turning the biggest liability into the sales pitch.

### A6 · phase 1 · phase-1

Narrow the launch to one beachhead domain — middle-school physical science/math paired with CS (where creation IS the curriculum standard) — seed the marketplace with 200-500 commissioned high-quality games via paid jams and a paid teacher-creator program before opening UGC, and fund a pre-registered external-validity study (pre/post transfer vs. comparison condition, ESSA Tier 3 minimum by end of Year 1). Do not use 'verified learning' or 'mastery' in marketing until it exists. 'All subjects' becomes a roadmap slide.

*Why:* Learning scientists' near-consensus: valid stealth assessment took years per construct for ONE first-party game; game-industry veterans: the flywheel starts from quality, not beginner volume; procurement: districts buy efficacy evidence, and the teacher-creator program simultaneously solves cold-start supply, review labor, and school champions.

### A7 · phase 1 · phase-1

Ship the school plumbing in Phase 1, not after: Clever/ClassLink/Google SSO and rostering, LTI 1.3/OneRoster, gradebook write-back, pre-signed DPA templates (SDPC/NDPA, NY Ed Law 2-d, plus GDPR DPAs with EU data residency and a self-hostable tier for Länder/académies), an AI-transparency one-pager for school boards, and time-boxed 5-lesson curriculum units mapped to named standards so making fits a 45-minute-period pacing guide. Target teacher overhead: under 15 minutes/week.

*Why:* Teachers in all four regions and every district CTO said the same thing: without rostering and a signed DPA the product legally cannot be used regardless of pedagogy, and without time-boxed units the making loop is confined to Friday afternoons. This is the difference between 200 schools being a plan and a fiction.

### A8 · phase 1 · phase-1

Rearchitect the knowledge-graph profile as student/parent-owned before first build: pseudonymous by default, full export and true deletion (including on leaving a school), contractual and technical guarantee that children's data never trains models, DPIA and UK Children's Code conformity, and a standards-mapped 'portfolio mode' export (NGSS, national curricula, exam boards) so lit nodes translate into currency teachers, parents, and admissions officers recognize. Add a parent dashboard: weekly plain-language digest (WhatsApp-deliverable), making-vs-playing breakdown, session budgets with save-and-summary endings.

*Why:* Privacy specialists called the profile 'the most detailed cognitive dossier on children ever assembled by a private company'; parents in every region noted the plan has a teacher dashboard and no parent anywhere in it — and in most of the world the parent, not the student, is the real gatekeeper of hours.

### A9 · phase 1 · phase-1

Make offline-first, mobile, and localized a market-entry gate: PWA client with downloadable game packs and offline play, a 'classroom pod' local server (Raspberry-Pi-class, Kolibri-style) that caches content and batches AI checks, touch-friendly phone creation with the AI doing the heavy typing, shared-device profile switching — and gate each country launch on native-language AI/gate plus knowledge-graph mapping to that country's actual exams (WAEC/JAMB, KCSE, ENEM, Tawjihi, JEE/CBSE, suneung, GCSE/Abitur/Bac). Add an 'exam mode' view and exam-season jams. State the China position explicitly (compliant 'coding education' JV or exclusion) and adjust DAU targets honestly.

*Why:* Every non-US cohort — teachers, parents, and students across Africa, MEA, LatAm, Asia, and Europe — independently identified the same three walls: shared low-end Android phones on metered data, exam calendars that ration every hour, and language. A comprehension gate in a child's third language tests English, not physics; a platform without an exam story loses every screen-time negotiation on three continents.

### A10 · phase 1 · phase-1

Split the brand and add sociality within safety limits: a kid-facing identity that never says 'learning' (dashboards, standards tags, and 'verified learning hours' visible only to adults; student profiles show creator status and cosmetics), plus Phase 1 co-presence — mobile play links that open instantly from a chat message, class-vs-class and school-vs-school remix leagues, and 2-4 person squad creation with COPPA-safe structured communication.

*Why:* Students in every region said nobody voluntarily uses something branded as learning ('a report card with an avatar') and nobody plays alone; kids create the way they play — together. Team competition against another school was the one format the panel's teens called socially safe, even braggable, and a pair explaining their game to each other is better pedagogy anyway.

### A11 · phase 3 · later

Cut Phases 3 and 4 from the operating plan (keep one vision slide at most) and open parallel low-friction wedges instead: after-school clubs, homeschool co-ops, libraries, EU Code Week/CoderDojo networks, and Asia's tutoring centers (hagwons/buxiban/coaching chains) — channels that buy in days, tolerate rough edges, and generate passionate early creators while district cycles grind. Repurpose the handheld concept as the classroom-pod offline kit sold to ministries/NGOs.

*Why:* Near-unanimous across experts, parents, and the heaviest-gaming students: learning-constrained games lose the sofa hour to entertainment, always, and districts reading Phase 3 see an attention company in an education costume. Meanwhile the Global South cohorts pulled Phase 4's hardware FORWARD as the thing that makes Phase 1 physically possible in their classrooms.

### A12 · phase north-star · phase-1

Reword the north star from 'verified learning hours' to a mastery-per-bounded-session metric (e.g., independently-validated knowledge-graph nodes lit per student per week, with session caps as a feature) and design natural stopping points into play.

*Why:* Safety specialists and parents converged: any hours-maximizing north star rewards the exact pattern regulators and families are fighting, and 'the AI verified your child learned something' will not survive a four-hour school-night session. The metric should make less time with more evidence the win condition.

## Three most surprising insights

1. Students revealed the real Phase 1 growth mechanic and it is not fun — it is permission. Kids in exam-driven households called the education branding 'a permission-laundering machine' (approvingly): a platform their parents classify as study is the only screen time they are allowed at all. The learning brand is simultaneously the product's biggest asset with parents and its biggest social liability with peers, which means the winning design is two-faced by construction — curriculum evidence for adults, creator status for kids — not a single averaged identity.

2. The comprehension gate got the panel's highest praise and harshest attack for the same mechanic: as a publish blocker it is the anti-Papert move that students will jailbreak and regulators will ban, but as a credential and provenance system it is the one asset Roblox structurally cannot copy — T&S veterans called it 'a rate limiter and provenance system disguised as pedagogy,' and exam-culture parents called it the first AI-in-education idea that fights cheating instead of enabling it. The entire fix is placement, not deletion.

3. The plan's most-mocked element — the Phase 4 handheld, laughed at by 20 of 25 Asian parents — was independently pulled FORWARD by African and Latin American cohorts as the thing Phase 1 actually needs: a sub-$200 offline classroom pod (local server caching games and batching AI checks) that ministries, NGOs, and donors — who, unlike the schools themselves, have budgets — would buy today. The 'founder fantasy' hardware is a Global South Phase 1 enabler wearing the wrong phase number.
